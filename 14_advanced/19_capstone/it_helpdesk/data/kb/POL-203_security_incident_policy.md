# POL-203: Security Incident Reporting

## What counts as a security incident

- You clicked a link in a phishing email, or entered your password on a suspicious page
- You received MFA prompts you did not trigger
- A company laptop or phone is lost or stolen
- You see someone else's confidential data that you should not have access to
- Malware or ransomware warnings on a company device

## What to do

1. **Report it immediately** through the helpdesk. Every security incident is
   handled as **priority P1** and goes straight to the IT Security team - it is
   not troubleshooted by the regular helpdesk.
2. Disconnect the affected device from the network (turn off Wi-Fi) if malware is suspected.
3. If your password may be known to someone else, change it at once or ask the
   helpdesk for a reset link.
4. Do **not** try to investigate or "clean up" on your own, and do not delete
   the suspicious email - Security may need it.

## What happens next

- IT Security contacts you within **1 hour**.
- For lost devices, the device is remotely locked and wiped.
- You will never be blamed for reporting an incident quickly. Delayed reporting
  of a known incident (more than 24 hours) is a policy violation.
